## 3. Change is evidence-gated, multidimensional, and reversible where appropriate

The reviewed relationship dimensions remain `TRUST`, `WARMTH`, `RESPECT`, `OBLIGATION`, `RIVALRY`, and `CAUTION`. They must not be collapsed into one affection, reputation, morality, or standing score.

A current-dimension transition is valid only when a typed relationship event records:

1. participants and branch applicability;
2. a specific cause/evidence class;
3. the affected dimensions independently;
4. durable history flags;
5. knowledge/player-visibility provenance;
6. repairability and whether repair changes current state;
7. explicit confirmation that repair cannot erase history.

Repeated gifts, repeated dialogue selection, resource dumping, passive proximity, or faction standing alone are forbidden as sufficient transition evidence. A loop that repeats the same evidence class cannot grind a dimension upward without a materially new event or changed context.

`RUPTURE` is not synonymous with relationship deletion. A rupture may raise `CAUTION`, lower one or more other dimensions, or close a specialist/optional route, but the history remains durable and shared foundational play remains available unless a separately reviewed contract says otherwise. Repair may restore or reframe current dimensions while retaining the rupture record.

Each template permits more than one lawful response and forbids a single canonical route. Trigger evidence may change current character state only through a later validated authored instance.

## 5. Agency, refusal, compromise, and consequence obligations

A principal character is not a reward dispenser or state machine controlled by player favor. Any later authored branch using these templates must preserve four agency rules:

- **refusal is representable:** a character may lawfully decline disclosure, labor, care, commitment, risk, or reconciliation when the reviewed constraints support it;
- **compromise is not surrender:** a compromise can retain disagreement, rivalry, caution, obligation, or unresolved truth;
- **change requires evidence:** a branch cannot flip an arc because the player crossed one relationship threshold or selected a persuasive line;
- **recovery preserves history:** later repair, apology, restitution, or changed behavior may alter current dimensions but cannot delete the causal event.

High-impact relationship or identity consequences require pre-commitment signaling, explicit affected goals, and at least one meaningful recovery/mitigation/alternative-content route unless irreversibility is itself separately reviewed and justified. No high-impact relationship branch may remove baseline movement, ordinary community interaction, public information, basic repair/crafting, cultivation, or other shared foundational play solely because a relationship worsened.
