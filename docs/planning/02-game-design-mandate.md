# Game Design Mandate — Seed

**State:** SEED

## 1. Reference Model

Stardew Valley is the primary reference model for the density of interlocking life-sim systems, long-term progression, optional activities, authored relationships, exploration, economy, and content layering.

It is a **complexity and coherence reference**, not a cloning specification.

Everfield must develop its own world, characters, content, visual identity, progression, mechanics, and balance.

## 2. Stardew Valley Is the Floor, Not the Ceiling

The target is a materially larger meaningful possibility space.

This does not mean merely increasing counts of crops, items, NPCs, quests, or maps.

The target is to create more viable player trajectories through interacting systems.

The ideal structure is:

> many overlapping games sharing the same world.

A player should be able to spend a substantial playthrough emphasizing a subset of major systems while still experiencing a coherent game.

## 3. Perceived Inexhaustibility

The game should be designed toward **perceived inexhaustibility** rather than literal infinite procedural content.

The player should repeatedly discover that the world and its mechanics are larger than expected.

Discovery should apply to:

- locations;
- mechanics;
- economy;
- production;
- relationships;
- narrative;
- progression;
- automation;
- secrets;
- system interactions.

It is acceptable and desirable that many players never see all major content in one playthrough.

## 4. Radical Sandbox Structure

The game should avoid a hidden assumption that the normal player must follow one dominant sequence of systems.

Potential emergent specializations include, without creating rigid classes:

- farming;
- ranching;
- fishing;
- mining;
- combat;
- exploration;
- crafting;
- artisan production;
- commerce;
- automation;
- industrial production;
- social progression;
- questing;
- collecting;
- property development;
- optimization;
- mixed lifestyles.

Later game-design agents must test whether these are actually viable and distinct rather than merely nominal options.

## 5. Systemic Breadth Over Decorative Breadth

The project should resist the temptation to use AI only to generate huge catalogs attached to shallow mechanics.

Desired growth is combinatorial:

```text
systems × content × interactions × progression × consequences
```

A piece of content should often participate in multiple systems.

For example, one crop might interact with:

- season/weather;
- soil or farming technique;
- crafting/cooking;
- market value;
- NPC preferences;
- quest requirements;
- production chains;
- automation infrastructure;
- collection goals.

The goal is dense possibility, not inert quantity.

## 6. Automation as Progression

Automation is a central progression fantasy.

Directional progression model:

```text
player labor
  -> better tools
  -> local infrastructure
  -> partial automation
  -> production systems
  -> logistics
  -> multi-site operations
  -> high-level capital allocation and system design
```

Most repetitive chores should have a theoretical automation path.

Automation should be expensive enough that manual action remains economically rational for a meaningful period.

Potential automation gates may include:

- currency;
- rare resources;
- infrastructure;
- relationships;
- specialist NPCs;
- quest chains;
- research/knowledge;
- magical or technological discoveries;
- land;
- maintenance;
- energy;
- logistics;
- prestige/faction access.

The exact fiction and mechanics remain open.

## 7. Automation Must Expand Ambition

Automation should not terminate gameplay by removing all meaningful action.

The design target is vertical growth in agency.

When a low-level task is automated, the player's attention should be freed for a higher-level challenge or opportunity.

Example conceptual escalation:

```text
water crops manually
  -> irrigation
  -> automated irrigation
  -> greenhouse management
  -> specialized production zones
  -> multiple properties/regions
  -> logistics
  -> high-value production chains
  -> large infrastructure projects
```

This progression must remain optional enough that players who enjoy direct labor are not forced into industrial play.

## 8. Late-Game Economy

Automation should provide one important answer to late-game currency inflation:

> capital buys freedom from repetitive labor and access to larger systems.

Later economic planning must explore other durable sinks as well, because one sink should not carry the entire late game.

Economic agents must simulate whether wealth remains meaningful across long playthroughs.

## 9. Core System Families to Explore

The final set is intentionally not fixed, but the planning program should at least investigate:

- farming;
- animals/ranching;
- resource gathering;
- fishing or equivalent skill activity;
- mining;
- combat;
- dungeons;
- exploration;
- inventory/items;
- tools;
- crafting;
- production chains;
- automation;
- economy/shops/trade;
- buildings/property;
- world time/calendar;
- seasons/weather;
- NPC simulation;
- relationships;
- dialogue;
- quests;
- story/world-state progression;
- events/festivals;
- cooking;
- collections;
- skills/progression;
- unlockable regions;
- late-game systems;
- UI/UX;
- audio;
- accessibility;
- persistence.

Planning agents are encouraged to propose additional system families if they create meaningful player possibility rather than feature-count inflation.

## 10. Narrative and Questing Are First-Class Projects

Narrative content must not be postponed until after mechanics are complete.

At least three large spaces should evolve concurrently:

- mechanical possibility space;
- world/content possibility space;
- narrative possibility space.

Separate agents should later design the interfaces between them.

Quest planning should eventually distinguish:

- structural solvability;
- progression fit;
- difficulty;
- grind/repetition;
- clarity;
- thematic quality;
- character consistency;
- player agency;
- reward quality;
- replay/path diversity.

## 11. Extensibility as a Product Property

Even if public modding is not initially promised, the internal design should favor cheap extension.

Adding a content object should rarely require invasive edits across unrelated systems.

Later architecture should favor explicit IDs, schemas, registries, events, commands, queries, validated references, and data-driven content where appropriate.

## 12. No Premature Completion Fantasy

The planning process must not attempt to enumerate every future possibility now.

The game is expected to evolve through repeated design and checkpoint loops.

The current mandate defines the direction of expansion, not the final catalog of features.

## 13. Product Quality Test

A useful future question for every major system is:

> Does this increase the number or depth of meaningful ways to live in Everfield, or does it merely increase content volume?

Systems that cannot answer that question should face stronger scrutiny.
